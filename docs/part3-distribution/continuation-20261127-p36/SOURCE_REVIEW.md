## Qualifications to the supplier audit

1. **Correction precision.** The bound in the new module is sufficient for its stated η-reserve. The manuscript separately gives a logarithm-squared bound for the modulus-prime mean correction and a divisor-bound estimate for deleting primes dividing the centre (`sieve-appendix.tex` lines 1126–1139). The combined elementary estimate here is an alternative, weaker bound at the required accuracy. It does not establish the sharper component estimates or the smoothed endpoint theorem at `argument.tex` lines 604–625. The exponent 599/1000 is greater than 153/256. The coarse envelope proves the elementary payment and cannot license enhanced distribution.
2. **Ordinary-root positivity and uniformity.** In audit line 26, the residual bound and positive limiting lower factor concern the frozen ordinary root density, normalized by multiplying by log N and dividing by the singular series. Its unnormalized scale is the singular series divided by log N. Positivity of the complete signed selected-policy main term still requires control of its actual frontier changes. Audit lines 29–31 use a cutoff depending on a fixed R. The definition of `PairRange` allows its positive epsilon to approach zero (`P2OriginalPairSourceReceiver.lean` lines 29–34). Reciprocal mass over the rough-prime interval can grow on the scale log(a/R.epsilon). Consequently the proposed bounds for each fixed R do not give a threshold uniform in all receivers. If positivity is sought only for one previously fixed receiver, the dependence can be retained explicitly. The new count reserve itself has the stronger uniform order because it uses a different, already-uniform density-to-count comparison.
3. **Frozen-splice proposal.** The generic splice theorem requires old and new ordinary prefix admissibility at *every* frontier state (`ActualFrozenFrontierDensity.lean` lines 148–166). The actual warm trace tests arrival against the incoming cap, then freezes the new cap for continuations (`P2LowerCapPlanIntegerRetention.lean` lines 92–110). Positivity requires a proof connecting those actions and the corresponding doubly marked residual levels, plus a weight comparison for the actual frontier enumeration. The cube-cutoff identity in audit line 30 is exact, but uniform use at ratio 3 still requires a compact range for the moved cutoff exponent and tracking its changed normalization factor. For a fixed R this can use a proved positive cutoff for changed prefixes. Audit lines 27–31 describe a potential proof. These steps receive no theorem credit here.
4. **Common-level obstruction.** On the stated rectangle the first candidate exceeds 0.5725, the second is at most 0.470625, and the constant candidate is 90621/160000. The larger mark therefore fixes the native level through Q=√(Np₂), up to the common buffer. An individual fiber has mass bounded by the bounded reciprocal mass of the smaller mark times 1/(p₂−1). This proves the obstruction for a uniformly bounded number of *exact native-level* fibers on that rectangle. Other regions, approximate frozen levels and reboxed coefficients require their own arguments. The manuscript fixes a common Q for each positive marked family and requires fixed margins on its boxes (`sieve-appendix.tex` lines 496–531).
5. **Expository shorthand resolved.** At initial hash `ccc7ed924a393b1a70c59a5cbd511272765faf8ac379bf6c87990855643e4672`, the summary at `RoundedCountReserve` lines 33–35 could be read as saying that raw density alone bounds the count. I compared the revised source to the review input. Its only change is the three-line docstring, which now follows lines 44–49 by retaining both losses **and** the signed rounded primitive term. The current rounding still holds the external pair fixed, so the conclusion supplies neither a joint labelled tensor identification nor a same-coefficient PP estimate.
