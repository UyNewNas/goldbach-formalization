In each asserted manuscript inequality, one C is chosen before level, frame, cutoff, length, coefficients and (on the holomorphic branch) ONBs. All level integers z>0, the identity and cusp-one observation, and every common complex sequence supported in L<n≤2L are allowed. The right side is precisely C(K²+L^(1+ε)/z) times the coefficient energy. The sets need not occupy the whole dyadic interval. We use the native spectral basis and native incoming channels throughout. The arbitrary holomorphic ONB can in particular be specialized to `fun j => formsBasis z (j+1)`.

**How the low real spectrum is paid.** `kernel_base_floor` proves K_it(q)≥K_0(q)/2 at q≥4, abs(t)≤2. With W(x)=exp(−q cosh x), F=xW, one has (2q−t²)W+t²F′≤2qW cos(tx) on x≥0. This follows from cos(tx)≥1−t²x²/2 and sinh(x)≥x. On [0,A] the boundary correction is nonnegative; integrability then suffices in the improper limit. The old imaginary kernel uses K_(2it), so the source calls it at t/2. On the old H=1 bump support ny∈(1,2) in the scaled frequency n, the kernel argument is q=2πny>4. The zero-order lower bound of the published probe supplies the common positive floor.

`packet_test` uses the old finite-hit spatial norm bound once for the whole Fourier polynomial. The scaled seed causes every frequency to have the same physical radial multiplier. Testing that vector against the actual discrete ONB and the full continuous projection gives positive squares of the **whole** marked row. The spatial coefficients are conjugated because this vector is in the conjugate-linear slot. The integral against the native projection has one factor 1/(4π) already inside `nativeMass`. Neither an independent test for each n nor a change of measure is involved. `manuscript_base` removes the multiplier on the low interval. `manuscript_base_three` joins the holomorphic base to it with one numerical constant. For high real heights `cumulative_majorant` integrates `reciprocal_cosh_le` on the unit interval ending at abs(t), enlarging to [1,K]; `diagonal_price` concerns the weight T dT only.

**Why the new holomorphic range has level saving.** `moving_strip` integrates the invariant Petersson density against the *existing* count of normalized-strip visits at either observed cusp. This gives the cost 2+4/(zY) above **arbitrary** Y>0, in every even weight. The former height-one cell estimate alone would not suffice. `strip_packet` identifies the q-coefficient on a horizontal line with its actual scaled unit-period Fourier coefficient and uses Parseval for *all* packet frequencies before integration in y. Thus the strip cost is independent of the number of Fourier marks.

Put k=2j+2, s=k−1, b=4πn, and take Y=s log(3/2)/(8πL). Then bY≤s log(3/2). On the omitted interval 0<y≤Y, y^(s−1)exp(−by)≤(3/2)^s y^(s−1)exp(−2by). The latter unscaled full mass is exactly 2^(−s) times the old mass by its old evaluated integral. The lost fraction is at most (3/4)^s≤3/4. This includes k=2. The surviving factor and the unscaled norm produce

    Γ(k) ∑_{n∈S} |R_f(n)|² ≤ [2s/π + 32L/(z log(3/2))] ‖f‖².

Here the native raw coefficient to `frameR` conversion and Γ(s+1)=sΓ(s) are both used; the unfolding factor is 4π. Testing the arbitrary packet functional on its own Riesz vector proves the same bound times ∑|a_n|² for its complete basis square. The cutoff is genuinely 2j+1≤K. Summing O(K) weights pays the manuscript right side if L/z≤K, K≤L^ε, or K≤2. In the intermediate range **K>2, L^ε<K<L/z**, the per-weight argument still repeats the length term. We do not claim its larger all-range estimate as the requested sieve.
