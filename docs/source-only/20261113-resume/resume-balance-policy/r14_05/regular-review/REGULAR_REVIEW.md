# Independent source review of the original regular spectral sums

The three native cumulative squares satisfy the manuscript bound for every positive level and both observation frames when (1\le K\le2). The holomorphic square also satisfies it at every (K\ge1) whenever (L/z\le K) or (K\le L^\epsilon). These are suitable scopes for source publication. The companion allowlist binds the exact author freeze, the eight Lean files, the author report and registry, and this review. Elaboration, kernel and axiom qualification remain outstanding.

**Measures and quantifiers.** For each fixed ε>0, `manuscript_base_three` chooses one positive constant before z, the observing frame, sign, K, L, S, the complex coefficients, and all holomorphic bases. S is any finite subset of the integers in (L,2L]. An arbitrary complex sequence on this dyadic block is obtained by taking S to be the full block or by extending by zero. The left endpoint is open. The case L=1/2 permits n=1, and an empty S gives zero. At z=1 both observations remain admissible. On each side the same sequence serves every spectral index and height, and may be selected afresh for each original level. The infinity and cusp-one observations are the normalized scalings of 1 and cuspOneMatrix. Both have period one and cusp density 1/z in the trivial-character case.

* The discrete sum uses the published full native cuspidal ONB with its multiplicities. The mask is λ≥1/4 and √(λ−1/4)≤K. The boundary λ=1/4 has order zero. The measure has weight 1/cosh(πt) and the positive and negative Fourier arguments have separate instances of the estimate. The ONB indexed by the formal statement is the chosen native basis. A unitary change within a fixed eigenspace preserves its summed squared functional.
* T08 `youngCuspEquiv` identifies the finite divisor/unit parameters with the full incoming cusp space. `nativeMass` already divides each squared native row by 4π. `lineTerm` divides once more by cosh(πt). The integral covers [−K,K] with both signs of t for either signed Fourier packet. `base_cumulative` returns local integrability as well as the numerical bound. The use of the native continuous projection takes the square of the marked functional before integration over t.
* The positive holomorphic square uses every member of any complete Petersson ONB at each k=2j+2 with k−1≤K. Its weight is Γ(k), and the source `frameR` equals √n times the raw Fourier coefficient divided by (4πn)^(k/2). Thus the printed conventional square with prefactor (k−1)!/(4π)^(k−1) and normalized raw coefficient ψ(n)/n^((k−1)/2) is 4π times the square in the terminal. This absolute factor is independent of k. The opposite-sign trace has the two weight-zero families only. At K=1 or 2 the only holomorphic weight admitted is k=2.

**Weight-zero proof.** The signed injection n↦±n preserves distinct frequencies and the ℓ² norm. The packet vector has coefficients conjugate to the desired sequence because the first inner-product slot is conjugate-linear. The old scaled seed makes its spectral multiplier independent of n. Applying finite-hit packing once to the whole Fourier polynomial gives the cost (8+32LH/z)Σ|a_n|². Discrete Bessel and the norm-one continuous orthogonal projection bound the probed squares separately. This reuses the spatial input of the published exceptional sieve, without using its exceptional lower bound at imaginary order or pricing a packet by the former fixed-mode constant (C_{z,n}).

For the base interval, write (W(x)=\exp(-q\cosh x)). Integrating the derivative of xW on [0,A], using sinh(x)≥x and cos(tx)≥1−t²x²/2, gives (2q−t²)∫W≤2q∫Wcos(tx). The endpoint contribution has the correct nonnegative sign at every A. Taking the integrable improper limit gives (K_{it}(q)\ge K_0(q)/2) for q≥4 and |t|≤2. The existing imaginary-order definition takes argument t/2 for this standard K order. The nonnegative H=1 bump has 2πy>4 on its support. The regular probe consequently has a positive absolute floor, half the previously proved zero-order floor. The inverse cosh weight is ≤1. Finite subsums give a summable discrete bound, while continuity and domination give the continuous bound. The stronger price before passage to the manuscript expression is (32(1+L/z)\sum|a_n|^2/\mathrm{baseFloor}^2). The conversion to (K^2+L^{1+\epsilon}/z) handles L<1 using z≥1.

**Petersson proof and weight summation.** The normalized strip count bounds its Petersson mass above Y by (2+4/(zY))‖f‖². The support indicator is unfolded against the full effective quotient and the invariant Petersson density. Horizontal Parseval bounds the sum of all selected squared positive Fourier modes at each y. The unit interval and the hyperbolic Jacobian y⁻² give the vertical mass (y^{k-2}e^{-4\pi n y}|\psi_f(n)|^2dy). Both steps use the same norm once for the entire set of frequencies.

Set s=k−1≥1 and (Y=s\log(3/2)/(8\pi L)). On 0<y≤Y the density at n is at most (3/2)^s times that at 2n. The latter full mass is 2^(−s) times the mass at n. The omitted fraction is ≤(3/4)^s≤3/4, including s=1. Using the prior exact identity `normalization_factor` at equal frequencies and Γ(s+1)=sΓ(s) yields the fixed-weight cost

    Γ(k) Σ_n |R_f(n)|² ≤ [2(k−1)/π + 32L/(z log(3/2))] ‖f‖² .

For arbitrary a the dual vector is v=Σ conjugate(a_n)Rvector(n). Evaluate the simultaneous restriction inequality on v, apply Cauchy in n, and cancel ‖v‖² when v≠0. Applying Bessel over the full ONB gives the same fee times Σ|a_n|², with no dimension or cardinality factor. This supplies arbitrary coherent rows beyond T21's fixed-one-mode statement.

Summing the positive weight squares through k−1≤K gives (ll (K^2+K L/z)\sum|a_n|^2) on all K≥1. Under L/z≤K the second term is ≤K². Under K≤L^ε it is ≤L^(1+ε)/z. For K≤2 the cost follows for all L≥1/2. In the remaining range (K>2,\ L^\epsilon<K<L/z), the factor K in front of the length term still requires payment. For example z=1, L large and (K=L^{1/2}) suffice to expose it when ε<1/2.

**Costs for the original bilinear return.** If the two post-removal rows have lengths M and L, one application on each side of the same positive measure gives by Cauchy

    Cε sqrt[(K²+M^(1+ε)/z)(K²+L^(1+ε)/z)] ‖a‖₂ ‖b_z‖₂ .

The length hypothesis for each row must hold in the given spectral family. Adding three such bounds costs at most three copies, or two on the opposite-sign branch. Squaring these bounds pays the corresponding absolute constant once in the squared price. In the actual physical application M=V−1/2, L=U/ν after removal, the common m-phase continues to use V, and the marked n-row retains its q-dependent Fourier and cusp factors. The costs of removal, mode summation, level Cauchy, cutoff and Bessel decay still belong to their respective steps. When (K_0=\max(1,2Y_0)>2), even the whole low interval of that particular physical decomposition extends beyond the three-family result. Its unbounded outer shells always require further estimates.

**Unbounded real heights.** For 2≤|t|≤K, the previous unit-band Mellin inequality holds for every (T\in[|t|-1,|t|]). Integration over that interval of length one and positivity on [1,K] give the new scalar `cumulative_majorant`. The elementary integral of T is (K²−1)/2. To turn this scalar bound into the arbitrary-row cumulative inequality one must still estimate the diagonal normalization and the weighted, same-observation geometric sum of the coincident and distinct frequency pairs in the physical trace. Existing compact trace and single-modulus operator identities do not supply the required entrywise oscillatory quadratic estimate. The chosen Mellin kernel has no established identification here with the printed pre-Kuznetsov kernel H. The published large sieve supplies a valid paper-level route; its full-range source proof on the named native measures remains open.
