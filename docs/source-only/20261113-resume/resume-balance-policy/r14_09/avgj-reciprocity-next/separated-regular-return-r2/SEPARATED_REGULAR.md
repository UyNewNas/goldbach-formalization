# Separating the regular return on the two Fourier marks

At a returned level d write M = 2ʲ − 1/2, C = πMY/Q and ζ = d/(4C). The four compact coordinates are u = Yx, v = m/M, w = n/M and ζ. The single fixed symbol used by the native period-64 sampler is

$$
F(u,v,w,ζ)=Φ(u)Φ(v)Φ(w)χ(ζ)Φ(\sqrt{vw}/(ζu)).
$$

For every physical mark, Φ(v) and Φ(w) are one. The actual support forces 16C/25 ≤ d ≤ 32C at any point where the switched test is nonzero. Hence ζ lies inside the χ plateau [1/8,8] there. Outside that plateau the entire reflected physical test is zero, and a level-only indicator sets every separated term to zero as well. The existing fixed radial χ cuts off the period copies outside the central cell. The source proves smoothness over the whole real four-dimensional space by localizing the divisions and square root on the support, then uses the existing period-64 lattice theorem to obtain for each integer r a finite bound for Σₖ ‖cₖ‖(1+|k₀|)ʳ. This bound depends only on r and the fixed symbol. The cₖ are its actual four-torus Fourier coefficients.

For each lattice mode k, the first leg on the conjugated input has additive parameter ω − k₁/(64M). The second leg has ω + k₂/(64M). These are fixed across the levels d. The remaining factor is the scalar cₖ e(k₃ζ/64) times the level indicator. The source exchanges these modes under the principal and odd-order Bessel transforms by integrated absolute domination. In the principal case, the entire sum of absolute coefficient-times-transform prices, multiplied by (1+|t|)³, is bounded by C₀(1+log²(2Y)) for Y ≥ 1. The choice of C₀ precedes Y, Q, d, both Fourier marks, the parameter t, and the additive phase.

The three slot theorems insert the original identity-frame basisR, the incident cusp nativeR with false frame, and the positive-weight frameR. Their original cosh(πt), 4π dt and Γ(2h+2) conventions are kept. The principal slot equality also covers a real spectral parameter equal to zero. The finite block identities apply to arbitrary coefficient rows, so multiplying either by an interval indicator implements a physical subinterval inside the original block. Their output is a sum of products of two independently shifted linear forms.

These are slot identities and a uniform principal transform price. Estimating the sum over the entire regular discrete spectrum and the integral over the incoming line uniformly in d, M and the phases still requires the native frequency-window large sieve. The remaining full regular bounds feed the error terms of Drappeau (4.29) and (4.30). [Drappeau, proof of Lemma 4.10](https://arxiv.org/html/1504.05549v4#S4.SS2)
