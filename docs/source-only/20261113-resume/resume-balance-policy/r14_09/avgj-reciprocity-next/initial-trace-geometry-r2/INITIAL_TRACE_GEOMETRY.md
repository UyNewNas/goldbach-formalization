# Arithmetic price of the first complete trace

Fix an integer subinterval [a,b) of the positive block [2ʲ,2ʲ⁺¹). On the original Γ₀(q) identity frame, form the complete positive trace for Φ(Yx), then contract m and n against conjugate(e(mω)) e(nω). The constant in the following bound is chosen before j, both endpoints, Y ≥ 1, the real level parameter Q, the additive phase and the finite set S of levels. With M = 2ʲ − 1/2,

$$
\sum_{q∈S}|Φ(q/Q)\,\mathrm{total}(q,a,b,Y,ω)|
 \le D_ε\big[(1+M)(1+Y)\big]^ε(M+Y)M .
$$

The exact cutoff identity first reindexes every geometric modulus as k = qd. The common cutoff in k is chosen from M and Y before q and ω. Both finite mark sums can then pass through the d sum. Because the first spectral coefficient is conjugated, the original smoothed inverse-sum kernel uses −ω, on the full positive Kloosterman sum with indices (m,n) and the original 1/k weight.

The support of each nonzero joint arithmetic sum forces 8πMY/5 ≤ k ≤ 16πMY. Four full-modulus dyads with lower endpoints πMY, 2πMY, 4πMY and 8πMY cover it. Their integral upper endpoints are the floors of the respective real cutoffs. The previously audited two-prefix estimate applies to each dyad and the full set of factorizations, including q = d. It allows arbitrary subsets of the latter. Taking the internal exponent ε/3 absorbs the three length/scale factors into the displayed budget. This estimate applies to the genuine complete three-sector trace after the interval contraction. Extracting the positive exceptional part in (4.30) also requires the native lower test and the bounds for the remaining spectra. [Drappeau, Proof 4.7](https://arxiv.org/html/1504.05549v4#S4.SS2)
