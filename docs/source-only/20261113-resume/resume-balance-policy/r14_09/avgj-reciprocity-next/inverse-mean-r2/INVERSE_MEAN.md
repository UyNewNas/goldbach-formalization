# Short inverse sums in the initial exceptional estimate

The terminal statements `initial_mean` and `initial_opened_mean` treat the two arithmetic sums in Drappeau’s proof of (4.30) at the manuscript’s common real phase. For every ε>0 there is one Cε for all integers K,M,N≥1, arbitrary finite subsets of the positive moduli up to K, two independent intervals with lengths at most M and N, and every real ω. The sum of U₂ is at most Cε(KMN)^ε K(K+MN). The same bound holds for the modulus sum of the absolute values of the original phase-weighted Kloosterman sums at these two prefixes. In particular the dyadic range of actual full moduli k=qd can be restricted freely, since each U₂ is nonnegative. In the final two statements the intervals are common to these moduli. The sharper dyadic harmonic-cost statement additionally allows the two interval endpoints to vary with k. [Drappeau, Proof 4.7](https://arxiv.org/html/1504.05549v4#S4.SS2)

## Simultaneous inverse-residue count

For K≤k≤2K, let u run over the units modulo k in [0,k). Place u/k+ω and its paired inverse u⁻¹/k−ω in arcs about integers with radii β and α. Modulus 1 and both resonances are included. Two translations of the canonical SL₂(ℤ) lift give a matrix with c=k, d=u−kr and a=u⁻¹−kℓ. Reading c and d modulo c recovers the pair (k,u). Use z=ω+iβ and w=ω+iα after enlarging each width to at least 1/K. The determinant identity gives the fourth centered entry as k(a/k−ω)(d/k+ω)−1/k. The four square entries of the real matrix from z to w imply 2 cosh d(gz,w)≤7k²αβ.

Two integral modular transformations, fixed once for the entire box, reduce z and w into the fundamental domain. The resulting heights y,v are at least 1/2 and at most 1/β and 1/α. Set R=6K√(αβ) and P=√(yv). After exchanging the two points by inversion if necessary, y≤v. For nonzero c the integral lower-left entry satisfies |c|≤R/P. For each signed c the d-interval radius is D=RP/v and the a-interval radius is A=RP/y. Two matrices with identical lower row differ by an integral left translation. Thus a belongs to one progression modulo |c| and determines the entire matrix for fixed (c,d). Here D≤R, DA=R² and P≥1/2. Summing the resulting (1+2D)(1+2A/|c|) bound over c costs O(R²(1+log(1+2R))). For c=0 there are two sign choices and one translated b-interval of radius RP per sign. The inequality RP≤6K supplies the separate O(K) term.

`rectangle_card` gives the bound 6000(1+log(1+12K))(K+K²αβ) at the *original* arc widths 0≤α,β≤1. Enlarging widths to at least 1/K adds at most 2K+1 to K²αβ. The constituent steps are recorded in `Part3OriginalInversePairMatrix`, `Displacement`, `Fibres`, `BallCount` and `Rectangle`.

## Returning to the physical sums

The integral layer i of a short sum of norm V is 1≤i≤⌈V⌉. Its frequency lies within 1/i of the nearest integer. For i=1 the half-circle bound suffices. For i≥2 the geometric identity and the sine chord give 4‖x‖ V≤2, and i≤2V. The layers stop at the actual length bound, including an empty prefix. In each of the two short sums the same phase ω is shifted with its original sign. Summing the box estimate over layers gives the dyadic bound 6000(1+log(1+12K))(KMN+K² H_M H_N). Strong induction at floor(K/2) sums all moduli, with a separate case for K=1. The inequalities H_n≤1+log n and 1+log t≤(1+1/ε)t^ε give the allowable choice Cε=12000·13^ε(1+1/ε)³. [Drappeau, original U₁ and U₂](https://arxiv.org/html/1504.05549v4#S4.SS2)
