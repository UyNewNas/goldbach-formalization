## Horocycle Riesz functional and the actual boundary

Fix a physical quotient vector V orthogonal to its constant and a source cusp b. Let A_b(y) denote the unit-period horocycle mean of conj(V) in the normalized b-frame. Height means for arbitrary L² representatives are identified almost everywhere. For a smooth compact height seed ψ let η(u) = exp(−u/2) ψ(exp u). Unfolding gives ⟨V,P_bψ⟩ = ∫ η(u) H_b(u) du with H_b(u) = exp(−u/2) A_b(exp u). The residual part of P_bψ pairs to zero against V. The generator Gram, column unitarity, and ordinary Fourier Plancherel therefore give |⟨V,P_bψ⟩| ≤ √2 ‖V‖ ‖η‖₂. The change of variables maps smooth compact log tests onto usable smooth compact positive-height seeds. By Riesz and uniqueness on compact tests, H_b belongs to L²(du). Its angular Fourier transform with the positive exponential convention is the candidate B_b(t) for the physical integral ∫ conj(V) E_b(1/2+it).

For V in the closed span of the centered zero generators, the actual Gram and scattering reflection fold the two terms of the zero-test column to identical integrals. The pairing then has factor 1/(2π) against the incoming Mellin wave. For arbitrary constant-orthogonal V the Riesz construction depends only on its projection onto that span, since the orthogonal complement annihilates every centered test. To claim a physical projection identity, one must prove the Riesz/Fourier coordinate agrees with these existing zero-test columns and extend on their norm closure. Closure of the image alone leaves a possible orthogonal component of a purported pointwise formula undetermined.

For actual compact finite packets, H_b vanishes above a finite log height. An economical proof uses the compact set K in the upper half-plane which supports each framed seed.
