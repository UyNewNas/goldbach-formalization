# Character and parity in the prescribed opposite-sign trace

The manuscript's direct caller specifies the trivial character on \(\Gamma_0(z)\), the two observing cusps \(\infty,1\), and the spectral arithmetic singleton \(s=1\). The correspondence fixes \(q_{\rm ABL}=1\) and conductor one. Its \(R(n)=\sqrt{|n|}\,\rho_{\rm ABL}(n)\) is the Whittaker-normalized Fourier coefficient. The regular branch takes \(q_0=1\) and \(\mu(1)=1/z\). The physical Eisenstein representatives have type `Gamma0OrbitSpace N → ℂ`. Their incoming index is `Parameters N`, a finite divisor and cusp-residue parameter. The original trace sums these parameters at fixed \(N=z\) and uses the infinity row for \(M\), the cusp-one row for \(N\), and signed integer arguments for both. Its cuspidal eigenbasis consists of scalar functions on the same quotient. Reflection symmetry of a Fourier coefficient under \(m\mapsto-m\) is unnecessary. The Bessel weight is even in real spectral height, and the source retains the value of every arithmetic coefficient at its own signed frequency and height. For the Young character expansion the signed lattice weight is \(\chi(c)\chi(d)\). Every Dirichlet character satisfies \(\chi(-1)^2=1\), so simultaneous negation of both row entries preserves the weight, including for odd \(\chi\). The conjugated square-level row action is diagonal modulo \(q\). Its determinant gives \(AD=1\bmod q\), hence \(\chi(A)\chi(D)=1\), and the actual Eisenstein function is invariant under \(\Gamma_0(q^2)\). The dilation \(B\) gives an identity with the finite physical cusp sum on \(\Gamma_0(N)\) under \(q^2B\mid N\). The scalar factor \(L(2s,\chi^2)\) arises because dilating both lattice coordinates by a common integer multiplies their character weight by \(\chi^2\) at that integer. Evenness of \(\chi^2\) alone would provide only the central parity condition. The determinant calculation proves the full trivial transformation law used here.
