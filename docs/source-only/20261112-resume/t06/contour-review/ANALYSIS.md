## Which integral is shifted

The physical scalar is F(s) = Mφ(s)[δ(h,g)mψ(s) + C(g,h;s)mψ(1−s)]. Here M is the literal log-coordinate Mellin transform, mψ(s) is the conjugate of Mψ(1−conj(s)), and the original geometric height measure is dy/y². The delta compares native cusp orbits. The scattering entry C is the unit-width horocycle mean at height one of the continued native family, minus its incoming wave. `scalar_on_line` identifies this exact scalar with `gramIntegrand`. `scalar_regular` uses `continuedEntry_regularity` and the entire Mellin factors, giving differentiability for Re(s) ≥ 1/2 except possibly at 1, including the point s = 1/2. `scalar_pole` gives the punctured-neighborhood residue at 1; this can be zero for particular test pairs.

* **Right, σ = 3/2.** `initial_spatial_gram` returns full-line integrability for every σ > 1, as well as its spatial inner product. Joint measurability is proved before the Fubini swap on `(carrierPushforward N).prod volume`. The majorant is the product of the absolute Mellin factor and the absolute compact-test pairing against the real-parameter Eisenstein series. The geometric unfolding and the representative of the quotient vector retain the actual frame and stabilizer multiplicities. This does not require a single Eisenstein series in spatial L².
* **Left, σ = 1/2.** `gram_critical_eq` factors the scalar as a Mellin wave times an actual boundary column. `gram_critical_integrable` applies Hölder on the whole t-line to two L²(dt) factors. One is the Fourier transform of the smooth compact seed in log coordinates; the other consists of two such waves multiplied by the cusp delta and an actual scattering matrix entry bounded by one. That entry bound follows from the native unitary identity. It does not use the proposed Gram equality. `physical_boundary` identifies this calculated column with the quotient pairing.
* **Continuous normalization.** `two_heights` identifies the sum over all native incoming cusp rows with the two scalar values at t and −t, using the actual adjoint reflection and scattering unitarity. `full_line_normalization` uses reflection and absolute integrability: dt/(4π) on the full physical product equals dt/(2π) on the full scalar boundary.
* **Residue and sign.** `residue_is_physical` identifies Mφ(1)·vol⁻¹·mψ(0) with the first vector paired against the projection of the second onto the actual quotient constant. The inner product conjugates the first vector. For a holomorphic function the contour gives right-minus-left = −i(top−bottom); the old finite pole kernel contributes 4 arctan(2T), tending to 2π. The constant projection is added in the spatial Gram.

## The horizontal bound

Fix any positive level N, arbitrary source/observing frames and the two smooth seeds. Each of the finitely many transformed compact unit horocycles remains in a positive compact height band. The primitive conductor and the level dilations are the original ones. The inverse divisor matrix is bounded at this fixed level through its genuine Euler determinant: the product over each divisor and its prime factors has positive uniform lower factors 1−1/p when Re(s) ≥ 1/2. Adjugate determinants have an elementary finite upper bound; the additional level power has a positive lower bound on the finite-width strip. This handles non-squarefree composite levels and the divisor 1. It does not imply a level-uniform large-sieve bound.

At conductor 1 the completed constant term contains both Λ(2s) and Λ(2s−1). On either high horizontal line the modified-theta Mellin integral is bounded uniformly on the required real strip by the two endpoint-weight integrals, and the explicit two rational polar terms stay bounded. The nonzero Fourier tail has a summable common majorant independent of t on the fixed height band. The other conductor branch uses the genuine completed character family. After uncompletion the cost is exp(π|t|)/(σ−1/2); the ζ bound applies in the open half-strip. This does not assert that the open-half-plane Dirichlet estimate extends uniformly to σ = 1/2.

## Why one real limit suffices

Write δ = σ−1/2 ∈ [0,1], T ≥ 1 and κ = π+2. Multiply by the entire function G_T(s) = exp[(κ/T)((s−1)²−1/4)]·[1+((s−1/2)/T)²]. On t = ±T the polynomial equals (δ/T)(δ/T ± 2i) and its norm is at most 3δ. The Gaussian is bounded by exp(−κT). The weighted horizontal integrand therefore has a bound of order exp(−2T), uniform in the horizontal coordinate. At the corner δ = 0 the polynomial vanishes and the fixed-height function is regular. No integration of 1/δ is involved.

On either vertical line the **entire** moving multiplier, including its truncation to (−T,T], is bounded uniformly in t and T. The absolute value of the polynomial is at most 1+(δ²+t²)/T² ≤ 3 on that interval; the source's triangle-inequality bound 5 also suffices. The Gaussian there has norm at most one. For each fixed t the indicator is eventually one and G_T(s) tends to one. The only integrable majorant needed for DCT is therefore a constant times the absolute value of the undamped line integrand.

Let r be the actual residue. Use U = regularPart(F,r)+r/s: the point s = 0 is outside the strip. Off s = 1 this equals F − r/[s(s−1)]. On both vertical lines the correction is continuous and bounded by 4|r|/(1+t²), including at t = 0. On either horizontal line of height |t| ≥ 1 it is bounded by |r|. Consequently U has absolutely integrable vertical sides and the same horizontal bound. Apply the weighted rectangle to U. Equality of its two unweighted vertical integrals, the original unweighted finite rectangle, and absolute integrability now give flux(U) → 0. The flux of r/s is O(1/T), so the original unweighted `edge` also tends to zero. The uncorrected regular part by itself can have a 1/t tail; it was not assumed absolutely integrable.
