# T25 — fixed marking, weight-two absolute modulus tail

Status: source-only proposal, no elaboration or kernel credit. The narrow arithmetic result supplies an absolutely summable majorant for the original positive weight-two geometric sum at infinity and cusp one. It does not identify that sum with a Hilbert-space reproducing vector.

The proof is in [ARITHMETIC.md](ARITHMETIC.md). For a fixed nonzero integer m it gives the same nonnegative B_m(d) for **all** second markings n (including zero) and all units twisting m, even where those choices vary with d. For every real δ<1/4 the positive-modulus series ∑ B_m(d)d^(−2+δ) converges. The proof in the note is stated for the needed 0≤δ<1/4; it also applies word for word to negative δ. The ordinary tail beyond D≥1 is O_m,δ(D^(−δ)) for 0<δ<1/4. The level does not enter the arithmetic majorant.

1. **Part3FixedMarkPrimeMoment.lean** derives the fourth-power estimate for the literal complete sum at a prime with nonzero first marking. Double additive orthogonality counts ordered pairs on the inverse graph. Away from zero pair sum, the unordered pair is determined by its sum and product; at zero the pairs are opposites. Unit dilation then isolates p−1 identical terms, including when the other mark is zero.
2. **Part3FixedMarkEulerMajorant.lean** uses the pinned literal CRT sum and unit scaling. Both local CRT parameters have their correct unit twists. The first marking is moved back to m, so no gcd with a varying inverse or hidden coprimality is assumed. Bad primes and **every higher prime power** use the triangle bound. The file constructs the multiplicative majorant and its weighted local prices.
3. **Part3FixedMarkEulerSummability.lean** bounds all partial sums by a finite smooth-number Euler product, using the mathlib lemma that requires only **local prime-power** summability. The good-prime contribution is a p-series of exponent 5/4−δ, while the higher powers contribute a uniformly geometric tail with leading exponent 2(1−δ). Only primes dividing |m| have a worse exponent-one price. Public interfaces are summable_majorant_price and physical_twisted_summable.
4. **Part3OriginalCrossCuspWeightTwoTail.lean** instantiates the original cuspOneMatrixSum and the original generalJ at order one. The old frame identity supplies e(n/z) and the unit inverse modulo d; the scale is C=d√z. The symmetric Poisson integral gives a global linear bound for J₁ with one finite, unevaluated Gamma constant. The terminal original_weight_two_absolute uses the actual argument 4π√(mh)/C and sums the norms over positive admissible d, with other terms exactly zero. It agrees in scale and arithmetic coefficient with the fixed-weight projection required by T21/T22; it does not replace the original compactly supported bareCuspTerm test with a Bessel function as a standalone identity.

All four new sources install under ImbalancedChen/ at their basenames, in the above order. The cusp-one source is an explicit overlay in the physical snapshot (not a root ImbalancedChen/ file there); the registry gives its origin and installation name. The exact verified byte hashes and source roles are in REGISTRY.json. Uncompiled historical second-moment, Fourier-inverse and totient modules were read for comparison; the new argument directly imports the old physical arithmetic/orthogonality, CRT and unit-scaling source instead of assuming any historical cancellation claim.

No uniform pointwise d^(3/4+ε), Weil bound, stationary-phase estimate, passage to the weight-two Hecke limit, all-cusp supplier, Petersson identity, positive trace inversion or parent spectral saving is claimed. The arithmetic majorant can be applied to subseries and finite fixed-level combinations; the geometry expressing another cusp as such a combination belongs to T21. The sharper coefficient 1/2 in the manuscript bound |J₁(x)|≤|x|/2 is not needed and not claimed by the Lean theorem.
