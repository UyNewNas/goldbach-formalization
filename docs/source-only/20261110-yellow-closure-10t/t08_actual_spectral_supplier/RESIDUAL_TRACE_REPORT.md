# T08 · The residual constant in the original normalized trace

At every positive level $N$, each normalized native Eisenstein function and every entry of its scattering matrix have the same residue at $s=1$,

$$
\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1} E_a(z,s)
=\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\Phi_{ab}(s)
=\frac{3}{\pi[\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z):\Gamma_0(N)]}
=\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma_0(N)\backslash\mathbb H)}.
$$

The point $z$, the source cusp $a$, and the observing cusp $b$ are arbitrary. The volume is the mass of the original quotient pushforward used by the Poincaré pairings. The signed nonzero-frequency packets in the manuscript both have zero orthogonal projection onto the resulting line of spatially constant functions.

## The pole under spatial observation

The original completed level-one series has residue $1/2$, independent of $z$. The new proof passes that limit through any unit-parameter continuous observation path in the upper half-plane, including every normalized cusp horocycle and its dilations. It rewrites $(s-1)E^*(z,s)$ by the existing entire Fourier part and the two explicit rational polar terms. On the circle $|s-1|=1/4$, the original compact-parameter, positive-height estimate bounds that function uniformly over the path. The maximum-modulus principle gives the same majorant inside the circle after the pole has been removed. Compact-path integration now preserves the limit at $1$.

For the ordinary conductor-one series, the genuine gamma and reciprocal zeta factors convert $1/2$ to $3/\pi$ by $\zeta(2)=\pi^2/6$. Every higher-conductor channel has zero residue there, including a primitive character whose square is principal. Its reciprocal $L$ factor is evaluated at $2$, and the completed character family has already been proved entire. The finite Young inverse has nonzero determinant at $\Re s=1$. Taking the limit of its actual finite sum gives the same residue for a given native cusp on every observation path. Observing at height one makes that value the residue of each outgoing entry in its row. The symmetry of the physical scattering matrix then identifies the residues across rows. ## Calibrating the common residue

The principal $q=B=1$ dilation expands into the original normalized cusp series with coefficient $w_a$ at $s=1$, where $w_a$ is that frame’s stabilizer width. The construction sends $(a,k)$, $0\leq k<w_a$, to the class of $T^k g_a^{-1}$ in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)/\Gamma_0(N)$. Equal cosets have the same bottom-row orbit. Within one orbit they agree exactly when the two offsets agree modulo $w_a$. Every coset is attained using the signed bottom-row representative of its orbit. Thus the sum of the widths is the actual index, which converts the level-one residue into $3/(\pi[\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z):\Gamma_0(N)])$.

The hyperbolic chart and measure are the same ones used in the physical quotient Hilbert space. The standard closed cell has $-1/2\leq x\leq1/2$ and $y\geq\sqrt{1-x^2}$. Integrating $dy/y^2$ over each vertical half-line gives $1/\sqrt{1-x^2}$. Its integral over the horizontal interval, including the original endpoints, is $\pi/3$. The existing finite-coset quotient mass formula then gives the volume in the displayed identity.

## The original pair of packets

For any quotient $L^2$ vector $V$, the orthogonal projection onto the span of the physical constant one is the constant function with value $\operatorname{vol}^{-1}\int V$. The computed cusp residue supplies exactly that coefficient. Each original signed nonzero Fourier seed integrates to zero after unfolding in its normalized observing frame. Finite summation keeps the same signed $n$-index, the original level-specific coefficient, and the common positive $m$-block and phase. Both original projections therefore vanish.
