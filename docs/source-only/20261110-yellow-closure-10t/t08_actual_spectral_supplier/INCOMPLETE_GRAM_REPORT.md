# T08 · Physical Gram matrix on compact zero-mode tests

At positive level $N$, let $P_{h,ψ}$ be the original sharp, normalized zero-mode Poincaré vector in the actual quotient $L^2$ space. For any pair of integral cusp frames $h,g$ and smooth compact positive-height profiles $ψ,φ$, the two endpoints of the contour have now been computed on those vectors.

With $U_a(t)$ the actual descended unit-leading Eisenstein function, put $V_{h,ψ,a}(t)=\int \overline{P_{h,ψ}}U_a(t)\,dμ$. This integral is absolutely convergent at every real $t$. The seven source files prove

$$
\lim_{T\to\infty} \frac{\mathcal E(T)}{2\pi}
=\frac1{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\sum_a V_{h,ψ,a}(t)\overline{V_{g,φ,a}(t)}\,dt
-\langle P_{h,ψ},P_{g,φ}\rangle+
\langle P_{h,ψ},\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathbf 1}P_{g,φ}\rangle .
$$

Here $\mathcal E(T)$ is the difference of the two actual horizontal edge integrals, multiplied by $i$, across the strip from $1/2$ to $3/2$, after removal of the computed simple pole. The desired spatial Gram identity still requires $\mathcal E(T)\to0$.

## Absolute pairing in the original quotient

The native Young dilation expansion descends to a continuous and measurable function on the spatial quotient at each regular contour parameter. At the critical line it is the same quotient function used in the original marked packet. A compact image in the observing frame bounds its modulus on the entire physical test support. Clipping it at that bound produces an actual quotient $L^2$ function. Every effective group summand and every unit-strip integral of the product is preserved by the clipping. The existing arbitrary-$L^2$ unfolding therefore applies to the original product.

Writing $J_ψ(s)=\int \overline{ψ(y)}\,y^s\,dy/y^2$, the result is the incoming term $\delta_{hg}J_ψ(s)$ plus $\Phi_{gh}(s)J_ψ(1-s)$. It holds for every continuous compact-height test, every $\Re s\ge1/2$ except the pole at $1$, and every pair of source and observing frames. The $\delta$ tests their actual cusp orbits. The two terms at the central parameter remain well defined.

## Initial Gram and critical normalization

The 27 October source already synthesizes a smooth sharp zero-mode seed on $\Re s=σ>1$ using $H_φ(s)=\int _{\mathbb R}e^{-su}φ(e^u)\,du$ with density $dt/(2\pi)$. The present Gram calculation integrates that synthesis against the second actual Poincaré vector. Its product majorant is the modulus of the pairing integrand with the real-axis Eisenstein state at $σ$, times the absolute Mellin transform in $t$. Both factors are integrable. The parameter dependence and quotient measurability give genuine joint measurability. The resulting single contour integrand is

$$
G(s)=H_φ(s)\bigl(\delta_{hg}J_ψ(s)+\Phi_{gh}(s)J_ψ(1-s)\bigr).
$$

The Mellin factors are entire by compact log-height support. The exponential Jacobian yields $J_ψ(s)=\overline{H_ψ(1-\overline{s})}$. On the critical line the two waves use opposite values of $t$. The actual unitary scattering matrix gives unit squared norm for each column and bounds its individual entries. Schwartz Fourier integrability gives $V_{h,ψ,a}\in L^2(dt)$ and $G(1/2+it)\in L^1(dt)$. The exact finite sum of physical products equals $G(1/2+it)+G(1/2-it)$. Integration gives the factor $1/(4\pi)$ for the full-line physical cusp sum. Young’s formal class normalization had already been obtained for the same cusp families. [Young, §8](https://arxiv.org/html/1710.03624v2#S8)

## Residue and finite rectangle

The physical pole contributes $r=H_φ(1)\operatorname{vol}^{-1}J_ψ(0)$. Unfolding against the constant function identifies this scalar with the actual projection pairing displayed above. Removing $r/(s-1)$ from $G(s)$ gives a holomorphic extension across $1$. The Cauchy rectangle has vertical sides $1/2$ and $3/2$. Both actual vertical integrals are absolutely convergent and this last factor tends to $2\pi$.
